# Usability check: the network

A ten-minute test with someone who has never seen the supply network. Don't explain anything; just watch and write down where they stop.

## Before

- A save with the first chain done but no route: a hall, one Farm with a hero posted and some grain stored, a discovered tavern that wants grain, the cart parked with a driver. (Debug screen: unlock the network, then build on a debug site and discover a debug tavern.)
- The phone on the map, Network toggle off, no sheet open.
- A notepad with three columns: **what they tried**, **where they hesitated (and for how long)**, **what they said**.

## The task

Hand them the phone and say only:

> "This town has a farm. Get its grain to a tavern."

If they ask a question, answer "What would you try?" Stop them after 5 minutes if they haven't finished.

## What to watch for

1. **Finding the network.** Do they go to the Expeditions screen's Network tab, tap the farm on the map, or try the 🕸️ toggle? How long before the first useful tap?
2. **Reading the picture.** On the Network tab, do they read the dashed line between the farm and the tavern as "not joined yet"? Do they tap the cart, the farm or the tavern?
3. **Route mode.** Once on the map in route mode, do they tap the pins in order (farm then tavern)? Do they understand the numbers on the pins? Do they find Save?
4. **Starting the cart.** After saving, do they find the cart's sheet and its Start button, or wait for something to happen?
5. **Confirmation.** Do they notice the solid line on the Network tab, the arrows on the route, the cart moving with its cargo icon?

## Afterwards, ask

- "What does the dashed line mean?" and "What does the solid one mean?"
- "What does the 🛒 badge on the farm mean?" (and any other badge they saw)
- "What would you do if the tavern left tomorrow?"

## Write down

- Every hesitation over 5 seconds, with the screen and what they were looking at.
- Every word they used for "delivery route", "stop", "cart". If they never say "route", the word may not be landing.
- Whether they finished, and how long it took.
